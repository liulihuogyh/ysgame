How to play

Every game here should be explainable in a sentence. This page is the longer version, for when something isn't obvious.

Before you start

  • No sign-up. Open the front page, pick a game, you're in.
  • Progress saves itself. Close the tab, come back tomorrow, the board is where you left it. The data lives in your own browser.
  • Phone, tablet or desktop. Portrait and landscape both work. On a computer you can also use the keyboard.
  • Sound can be turned off. The speaker icon in the top bar. Your choice is remembered.

Sudoku: the rules

The board is a 9×9 grid, divided by heavier lines into nine 3×3 boxes. The goal: fill every empty cell so that each row, each column and each 3×3 box contains the digits 1 to 9 exactly once.

The numbers already on the board are fixed. You can't change them, and you don't need to. Every valid puzzle has a single solution — you never have to guess, only reason.

What the reasoning actually looks like

Take a row that already shows 1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8 and 9. Two cells are left, so they must be 2 and 6. Now look at the columns those cells sit in: one of the columns already has a 2, so that cell has to be 6, and the other is 2. That's the whole game — narrowing until only one answer is left.

One tip: don't start filling. First scan the rows, columns and boxes that already have the most numbers — they usually hand you a few cells for free.

Controls

Mouse or finger

  • Tap an empty cell, then tap a number from 1 to 9
  • Undo steps back one move, Erase clears the selected cell
  • Hint fills one cell for you — three per game
  • Check marks wrong cells in red. It tells you where, never what

Keyboard

  • 1 to 9 to enter a number
  • Backspace or Delete to clear the cell
  • Ctrl + Z to undo

2048: the rules

A 4×4 board, starting with two tiles. Slide up, down, left or right and every tile travels as far as it can in that direction. Two tiles with the same number merge into one, doubling the value. Each slide adds a new 2 (sometimes a 4) to an empty square.

Each tier has its own goal: Easy 1024, Medium 2048, Hard 4096. Reach it and the round is won, but you can keep going — 8192, whatever you like. Harder tiers also spawn 4s more often. Nothing here is timed. The game ends only when the board is full and no direction changes anything.

Controls

Phone or tablet: swipe anywhere on the board. Short swipes count.

Keyboard:

  • ↑ ↓ ← →, or W A S D
  • Ctrl + Z to undo, up to twenty moves back
A small tip: park your largest tile in one corner and only slide along the two directions that touch it. Slide in all four directions and the big numbers drift into the middle.

Your score is the sum of every tile created by merging. The best score is kept separately and survives a new game. Close the tab whenever you like — the board is saved and picks up where you left it.

Minesweeper: the rules

A patch of ground with mines hidden under some of the squares. Open a safe square and the number on it tells you how many mines touch it, counting all eight neighbours. Open every safe square to win. Open a mine and the round is over.

Your first click is never a mine, and the 5×5 area around it is cleared for you, so you always start with a little open ground instead of guessing blind.

Three boards

LevelBoardMinesGood for
🌱 Easy9 × 910A few quiet minutes
🌳 Medium13 × 1325A little more to think about
🔥 Hard16 × 1640Sitting down to reason it out

Controls

Mouse: left-click to open, right-click to flag. Click an open number again and, if the flags around it match the number, the rest of its neighbours open at once — that is the chord, and it saves a lot of clicking.

Phone or tablet: tap to open, press and hold to flag. You can also tap 🚩 Flag to stay in flag mode, plant a few flags, then tap it again to come out.

Keyboard: F toggles flag mode, Tab moves between squares, Enter opens one.

A small tip: you do not need a flag on every mine. What matters is counting how many covered squares a number still touches. If that count equals the number, all of them are mines. If it is already zero, all of them are safe.

The timer starts on your first click and stops when the last safe square is open. Each board size keeps its own best time, and a new game does not clear it. Close the tab whenever you like — the board is saved.

Jigsaw: the rules

A picture has been cut into pieces and shuffled. Put every piece back where it belongs by swapping two at a time, until the picture joins up again. There is no clock and no penalty — take your time.

Three sizes

SizePiecesGood for
🌱 Easy 3 × 39Two or three quiet minutes
🌳 Medium 4 × 416Sitting down with the picture for a while
🔥 Hard 5 × 525When you want a proper sit-down

Controls

Tap two pieces: tap one, it lifts with a ring around it, then tap another and the two trade places. Tap the same piece again to let it go.

Peek: the 👁 Peek button lays the whole picture over the board for four seconds, then puts itself away. Use it as often as you like.

A small tip: start with the landmarks — the sun, the moon, the plant pot, the window frame. Save the large areas of sky and wall for last. Nothing is irreversible: two more taps put a piece back.

The clock starts on your first swap and stops when the last piece lands. All three sizes keep separate best times. The board is saved if you close the tab; once a picture is finished, opening the page starts a new one.

Match three: the rules

An 8×8 board with six kinds of candy. Swap two neighbouring candies so that three or more of a kind line up — across or down — and they clear together. Everything above falls down and new candies drop in at the top.

Each tier sets its own limit and goal: Easy 35 moves / 1000 points, Medium 30 / 1500, Hard 24 / 2000. Each candy cleared is 10 points. If the falling candies make a new line, that clears too, and each extra cascade doubles the points. Run out of moves before the goal and the round ends.

Controls

Swapping: tap one candy, then a neighbour above, below, left or right. If the swap makes no line, the two slide back and no move is spent.

Hint: tap 💡 Hint and one workable pair pulses gently on the board.

A few small tricks

· Work from the bottom of the board — falling candies are more likely to line up again.

· One line of four or five is usually better than three separate matches of three.

· If the board has no possible move left, it reshuffles itself. You cannot get stuck.

No rush: there is no timer and no penalty for thinking. A careful tap beats three hopeful ones.

Every move is saved, so closing the tab keeps the board. Your best score is kept separately and survives a new game.

Snake: the rules

A 17×17 board. The snake never stops moving in its current direction. Every fruit it eats makes it one segment longer, adds a point, and nudges the speed up. Easy, Medium and Hard change the starting speed, and each tier keeps its own best score. Hit a wall, or bite your own body, and the round ends.

Controls

Computer: arrow keys or WASD to turn, Space to pause and resume.

Phone or tablet: swipe across the board in the direction you want, or use the four buttons below it — one hand is enough.

Starting and pausing: the snake waits until you press a direction or tap Start. Switching tabs pauses it automatically.

A small tip: do not hug the walls — leave a square of room. Turn a little early, since the snake only turns once per step. The longer you get, the more space you need to turn around in.

Your best score is kept in the browser and survives a new game. The round does not restart itself — press Play again when you want another go.

Sokoban: the rules

Every level has boxes (brown blocks) and dots (dashed circles). Push all the boxes onto the dots and the level is done. You can push but never pull, so a box shoved into a corner stays there — undo is one tap away.

Controls

Computer: arrow keys or WASD to move, U to undo, R to restart the level.

Phone or tablet: swipe across the board, or hold one of the arrows below it — holding keeps you walking, so you do not have to tap every step.

Undo: every step is remembered. Undo walks back one move and takes the count with it.

A small tip: the classic way to lose is a box in a corner — once it touches two walls it will never move again. Before you push, check where you will be standing afterwards: the hard part of Sokoban is never the push, it is getting around to the other side.

Three tiers — Easy, Medium, Hard — fifteen levels each, 45 in total, getting harder as you go; the hard tier asks you to plan the order of several boxes. Your best move count is kept per level, and cleared levels get a small dot under the number.

Number slide: the rules

The board holds shuffled numbers and exactly one gap. Put the numbers in order — 1, 2, 3 and so on — and leave the gap in the last square to finish. A number can only slide into the gap beside it; nothing jumps, nothing gets picked up.

Controls

Computer: click a number next to the gap and it slides in. Arrow keys work too (the direction the number travels), and U or Z undoes.

Phone or tablet: tap the number, or swipe across the board — swipe in the direction you want the number to move.

Size: switch between 3x3, 4x4 and 5x5 at the top. Each size keeps its own best score.

A small tip: do not chase one number at a time. Solve the top row first, then treat it as finished and leave it alone. Work down row by row; on the last two rows, use the gap to rotate numbers around in a small circle so you do not wreck what is already placed.

Every round starts from a solved board and is shuffled by hundreds of random legal moves, so it is always solvable — you will never meet a dead position. Close the tab halfway through and it picks up where you left off.

Tetris: the rules

Seven shapes drop from the top of the board, one at a time. Move them left and right, turn them, and let them settle neatly at the bottom. Fill a whole row and it clears, everything above drops down a line, and clearing several rows at once scores more. When the stack reaches the top and a new piece won't fit, the round is over.

The next piece is shown beside the board, so you can plan where it goes. There is no clock — take as long as you like.

Controls

Computer: ← → to move, ↑ to rotate, ↓ to soft-drop, Space to hard-drop straight to the bottom. The Pause button stops and resumes at any time.

Phone or tablet: use the buttons below the board — left, rotate, right and hard-drop — or swipe across the board to slide a piece.

Mouse: the same on-screen buttons work with a click.

Three speeds

🌳 MediumSteadyYou have the feel of it and want longer clears
🔥 HardFastLittle time to think — decide as it falls

The three levels mainly set the starting fall speed, and also change the speed floor, how much faster each level gets, and how many lines you need to level up. Pick one before you begin.

A small tip: keep the stack flat instead of building upwards too soon. Leave a vertical well open and wait for the long bar — four rows at once is worth far more than four separate singles.

Score adds up per cleared row, with more for bigger clears. Your best score is kept separately and survives a new game. Close the tab and the board is saved, so you can pick it up later; pausing leaves it exactly where it was.

Breakout: the rules

A paddle sits at the bottom and a ball bounces around the screen. Slide the paddle to catch the ball and send it back up; most bricks break on impact, while darker, tougher ones need two hits. Clear all the bricks to finish the level and move to the next. Let the ball slip past the paddle and you lose a life; run out of lives and the round ends.

Some bricks drop a power-up when they break. Catch it with the paddle and it kicks in for a while: a wider paddle, extra balls, or a slower ball.

Controls

Computer: ← → or A D to move the paddle, or just move the mouse across the board. Space or a click launches the ball; the Pause button stops and resumes at any time.

Phone or tablet: slide a finger across the board and the paddle follows; tap the board to launch the ball.

Mouse: move the mouse inside the board and the paddle follows.

Three levels

LevelBall & paddleGood for
🌱 EasySlow ball, wide paddleLearning the bounce angles
🌳 MediumModerate ball and paddleCatching it reliably, aiming your shots
🔥 HardFast ball, narrow paddleQuick reactions and a bit of anticipation

The levels mainly change the ball's starting speed and the width of the paddle, and also set the number of brick rows, the share of tougher bricks, how often power-ups drop, and the speed gained per level. Clear a level's bricks and the next one begins.

A small tip: watch where the ball lands on the paddle, not just the ball itself. Hitting off-centre changes the rebound angle — that's how you reach the bricks tucked in the corners.

Every brick is worth points, and the score carries on into the next level. Your best score is kept separately. The current level, lives left and difficulty are saved automatically, so closing the tab keeps your place; pausing holds the board as it is.

Link-up: the rules

The board is covered in pairs of matching tiles. Tap two alike and, as long as you can join them with a line that turns no more than twice and crosses no other tile, the pair clears. The line is allowed to run around the outside of the board, so a tile in the corner isn't necessarily stuck. Clear every tile to finish.

There's a countdown and a score on screen — clear quickly and keep the streak going for a better one. If you can't see a move, Hint finds a pair for you and Shuffle deals the remaining tiles again. Both are limited per round, so save them for when you're truly stuck.

Controls

Mouse or finger: tap one tile, then another of the same kind. If they connect, they clear; if not, the selection drops and you simply try again.

Pausing: the Pause button stops the board and the clock together; Resume picks up where you left off.

Three levels

LevelTiles & timeGood for
🌱 EasyFewer tiles, generous time, more hints and shufflesLearning what a valid link looks like
🌳 MediumMore tiles, less time, fewer hintsYou have the feel of it and want a clean clear
🔥 HardMost tiles, the tightest clock, the fewest hintsSharp eyes and quick hands

The levels mainly add tiles, shorten the clock, and cut the number of hints and shuffles. Pick one before you start; you can switch mid-game, though that starts a fresh round.

A small tip: sweep the corners and edges first — tiles with only one or two open sides are the ones that get stuck, and clearing them early opens up the middle. Hold off on Hint until you're genuinely stuck.

The remaining tiles, score, time left and difficulty are all saved, so closing the tab keeps the round and pausing holds the board exactly where it was. Each level keeps its own best result, which survives a new game.

Memory match: the rules

Every card starts face down. Flip two at a time: a matching pair stays up, a mismatch turns back after a short pause. Remember where things are and collect all the pairs to finish. The game counts your moves and your time — fewer moves is the nicer result.

Controls

Mouse or finger: tap any two face-down cards. A match stays face up; a mismatch flips itself back, and you try the next pair.

Pausing: the Pause button stops the board and the clock; Resume carries on.

Three levels

LevelGrid & paceGood for
🌱 Easy4×4, slow flip-back, no clockGetting used to remembering positions
🌳 Medium4×5, more cards, faster flip-backYou remember well and want fewer moves
🔥 Hard6×6, fastest flip-back, with a countdownRemembering fast and flipping faster

The levels mainly grow the grid and shorten how long a mismatch stays visible; Hard adds a countdown too. Pick one before you start, or switch mid-game for a fresh round.

A small tip: don't flip at random. Each time you turn a card, quietly note the ones beside it; work one patch of the board at a time and collect the pairs you already know. It saves far more moves than scanning the whole grid.

The cards, move count, time and difficulty are all saved, so closing the tab keeps the round and pausing holds the board as it is. Each level keeps its own best result, which survives a new game.

Gomoku: the rules

A 15×15 board. You play black and move first; the computer plays white, and you take turns placing a stone on an intersection. First to get five in a row — across, down or diagonally — wins. Stones don't move and nothing gets captured, so it's all about planning ahead.

Undo takes back one turn, which is two moves — yours and the computer's — returning the board to just before you played. If you're unsure where to go, Hint suggests a spot.

Controls

Mouse or finger: tap near an intersection and the stone lands there; you don't have to be precise.

Pausing: the Pause button stops the game and Resume carries on with the same board.

Three levels

LevelThe opponentGood for
🌱 EasyLooks only one step ahead, mostly builds its own lineLearning to spot and block a three
🌳 MediumBlocks obvious threats and makes its ownA proper back-and-forth game
🔥 HardLooks further ahead, steady on both attack and defenceWhen you want to think each move through

The three levels change how strong the computer is, from short-sighted to looking several moves ahead; the board and the rules are identical. Pick one at the start, or switch mid-game for a fresh round.

A small tip: before extending your own line, check whether the other side has an open three or four. A move that blocks them and continues your line at once is the best kind of move.

The board, whose turn it is, the difficulty and the result are all saved, so closing the tab keeps the game and pausing holds the board as it is. Your record for the level is kept separately and survives a new game.

Mario: the rules

A side-scrolling level that keeps moving right with you. Jump the gaps in the ground, stomp the little creatures from above, pick up coins, and hop over the pipes in the way. Touch the flagpole at the end to clear the level — there are three levels, one after another.

Falling into a gap or bumping into a creature costs a life; when the lives run out the round is over. Stomping creatures and collecting coins both score, and the lives left and your score are shown on screen.

Controls

Computer: arrow keys or A D to move, Space or ↑ to jump. The Pause button stops and resumes at any time.

Phone or tablet: three on-screen buttons below the level — left, right and jump — so one hand is enough.

Three levels

The levels mainly change the running speed, how crowded the creatures and gaps are, and how many lives you start with; the three levels themselves stay the same. Pick one before you start, or switch mid-game for a fresh round.

Maze: the rules

The site generates a "perfect maze" on the spot with a randomised depth-first search — every pair of points is joined by exactly one path, so there are no nested dead ends. You start at the top-left and walk one cell at a time to the exit at the bottom-right. Clear a level and the maze regenerates larger; the deeper you go, the more it winds.

Controls

Computer: arrow keys or WASD to move, Space to pause and resume; just press a direction to get going.

Phone or tablet: swipe one step in any direction on the maze; a tap also starts you off, and another tap resumes. There is a Hint button at the bottom that lights up the shortest route to the exit.

Three levels

LevelWhat changesGood for
🌱 Easy11×11 grid, no time limit, unlimited hints, low score base, small step penaltyA relaxed stroll
🌳 Medium17×17 grid, 90-second limit, 8 hints, moderate score and step penalty, time bonusWhen you want a little pressure
🔥 Hard25×25 grid, 120-second limit, only 3 hints, high score base, heavy step penalty, bigger time bonusPlayers happy to get lost

The three levels set the grid size, time limit, hint count and the scoring formula (level score = score base × level − steps × penalty + time left × bonus). Switch at any time before you start.

A small tip: stuck? Tap Hint — the purple glow is the shortest path the game computed. Easy gives unlimited hints; Hard gives only three, so spend them well.

The maze, level, steps and time left are all saved, so closing the tab keeps your place and pausing holds it as it is. Your best score is kept separately and survives a new game.

Battle Tank: the rules

You drive a tank that moves in four directions and fires. Brick walls can be blown open, steel walls cannot; at the bottom sits your base (the eagle), and you only get the next level if you keep it standing. Enemy tanks keep spawning from the top in waves — clear the current wave to advance, and later levels bring more of them.

Controls

Computer: arrow keys or WASD to move, J or Space to fire, P to pause and resume.

Phone or tablet: a four-way pad at the bottom-left moves you, a fire button at the bottom-right — workable with one hand or two.

Star Fighter: the rules

Your ship sits at the bottom and fires automatically; steer left and right (with a little up and down) using the arrows or your finger, and dodge the enemy squadrons diving from above and the bullets they spray. Destroying a ship scores points, and sometimes drops a power-up: P for more firepower, S for a shield, W for a wingman. Run out of lives and the round ends; clear a wave to reach the next.

Controls

Computer: arrow keys or WASD to move (the ship fires on its own), P to pause and resume.

Phone or tablet: drag a finger across the screen and the ship follows; it fires automatically, so you only worry about dodging and grabbing drops.

Bomber: the rules

On a grid map, steel walls never break and soft walls do. Drop a bomb and, after a few seconds, it bursts in a cross, chain-detonating other bombs and clearing soft walls; behind a soft wall hides the exit that clears the level — reach it and step on to win. Enemies patrol or chase you, and touching one costs a life; you get 3.

Controls

Computer: arrow keys or WASD to move, Space or J to drop a bomb, P to pause and resume.

Phone or tablet: a four-way pad at the bottom-left moves you, a bomb button at the bottom-right drops one.

Number Link: the rules

Scattered across the grid are pairs of matching numbered endpoints. Join each pair with a line, never let two lines cross, and fill every empty cell with those lines to clear the board. Puzzles are made by cutting a Hamiltonian path that covers the whole grid into segments, so every board is guaranteed to have a solution — you will never meet an impossible one.

From Easy to Hard the grid grows, the number of pairs rises, hints get scarcer, and a countdown appears. Connect every pair and fill the board to move on; there is no cap on levels.

Controls

Computer: press and drag the mouse from one numbered endpoint to its match, and the cells you pass through become a line — release to finish it. Erase a whole line with the right mouse button, or click Undo to step back. You can also draw with the keyboard: Arrow keys / WASD move the cursor and Enter starts and ends a line; Space or P pauses. The Undo, Clear and Hint buttons are always there.

Phone or tablet: drag a finger from one number to its match to draw the line; press and hold a segment to erase it, or use the buttons below the board to undo, clear or get a hint.

Three levels

LevelWhat changesGood for
🌱 Easy5×5 grid, 4 pairs, 5 hints, no time limitLearning the "no crossing, fill the board" idea
🌳 Medium7×7 grid, 6 pairs, 3 hints, 180-second limitYou have the feel of it and want to think as you draw
🔥 Hard9×9 grid, 8 pairs, only 1 hint, 120-second limitSitting down to plan the whole board

The levels mainly set the grid size, number of pairs, hint count and whether there is a clock. Pick one before you start, or switch mid-game for a fresh board.

A small tip: don't rush to join the nearest pair. Leave a way out for the corners and keep each line inside its own patch of cells; the whole board has to be covered, so no cell can be left empty — the sooner you hand a large empty area to one line, the easier the rest becomes.

The current board, level, lines drawn, hints left and difficulty are all saved, so closing the tab keeps your place (resume where you left off). Your best score is kept separately and survives a new game.

Sky Bird: the rules

You get one button: tap it to flap upwards, then glide back down. Thread the bird through the gap between each pair of top-and-bottom pipes; touch a pipe or the ground and the run is over. It is an endless score chase — every pipe cleared is a point.

The three levels change gravity, the size of the gap, how fast the pipes scroll and how close together they are, and all of them speed up as your score climbs. You get a single life at every level.

Controls

Computer: Space, ↑ or W to flap, once per beat; P to pause and resume. There is no steering, only timing.

Phone or tablet: tap anywhere on the screen to flap; one tap, one beat. Light, even taps are steadiest.

The current run, score and difficulty are saved automatically, so closing the tab lets you pick up where you left off. Your best score is kept separately and survives a new game.

Dino Dash: the rules

Tap or press a key to make the little dinosaur jump — hold it to jump higher — over the cacti and birds streaming towards you; a duck key also lowers its profile on the ground. The pace climbs steadily the further you run, and your score is simply the distance in metres (1 metre = 1 point). Every 350 metres the scenery fades between day, dusk and night palettes and a milestone banner rolls past. Touch an obstacle and the run ends; it is an endless chase for distance.

Controls

Computer: Space, ↑ or W to jump, hold the key to go higher; ↓ or S to duck, P to pause and resume.

Phone or tablet: tap anywhere on the screen to jump, hold the touch down for a higher arc; ducking is a keyboard control.

Reversi: the rules

An 8×8 board. You play black and move first, the computer plays white, and you take turns placing a disc. Any of the opponent's discs sandwiched between your new disc and another of yours flip over to your colour, in every direction at once. If you have no legal move, your turn is skipped automatically; when the board is full or neither side can move, the side with more discs wins.

Legal squares are highlighted as you play, and there are Undo and Hint buttons. The rules and the board are identical at every level — only the computer's strength changes.

Controls

Computer: click a highlighted square to place a disc; click Undo to take back a turn (one undo rewinds both your move and the computer's) or Hint for a suggested move. Keyboard works too: Arrow keys move the cursor, Space or Enter places a disc, U undoes, H hints and P pauses.

Phone or tablet: tap a highlighted square to place a disc, or tap the buttons below the board to undo and get a hint.

Three levels

LevelWhat changesGood for
🌱 EasyPositional-weight greedy play with 30% random noise (grabs corners, avoids giving them away)Learning flips and why corners matter
🌳 MediumWeight table + mobility + depth-2 minimaxA proper back-and-forth game
🔥 HardWeights + stable discs + mobility + depth-4 minimax with alpha-beta pruningWhen you want to think each move through

The three levels change how the computer picks its move, from a greedy guess to searching several plies ahead; the board and rules stay the same. Pick one at the start, or switch mid-game for a fresh round.

A small tip: corners can never be flipped, so they are worth grabbing — but the squares beside a corner often hand it to your opponent. Early on, having fewer discs but more legal moves is usually the stronger position.

The board, whose turn it is, the difficulty and the result are all saved, so closing the tab keeps the game and you can resume where you left off. Your record is kept separately and survives a new game.

Stack Jump: the rules

A row of isometric blocks stretches up and to the right, and you stand on one of them. Hold to charge, release to leap, and land cleanly on the next block to score; hit the little mark dead centre and it is a perfect landing, and consecutive perfect landings build a combo that is worth more and more. The longer you hold, the farther you fly — overcharge and you sail straight past the block, and one miss ends the run. You get a single life.

The three levels change the size of the blocks, the gaps between them, whether they slide from side to side, how forgiving a perfect landing is, how fast the charge builds, and — on Hard — a crosswind that nudges you off course.

Controls

Computer: hold the left mouse button or Space to charge and release to jump; a dashed ring shows roughly where you will land while charging. P pauses and resumes, and Space also starts or restarts a run.

Phone or tablet: press and hold anywhere on the board to charge, then let go; a tap also starts a run.

Three difficulty levels

LevelWhat changesGood for
🌱 EasyBlock width 92, gaps 105–155, no moving blocks, perfect window 30%, charge rate 1.0, no windLearning the charge; wide blocks close together
🌳 MediumWidth 72, gaps 130–200, 20% of blocks slide, perfect window 18%, charge 1.35, occasional narrow blocksYou have the feel of it and must track moving blocks
🔥 HardWidth 54, gaps 170–250, 45% of blocks slide faster, perfect window 10%, charge 1.75, crosswind drift, more narrow blocksA steady hand, plus reading the wind

Every level gives exactly 1 life; pick one before you begin, or switch mid-game — that starts a fresh run and your progress is not kept.

A small tip: watch the dashed landing ring while you charge — don't just hold it down all the way, or you'll overshoot the block. Dead centre is worth far more than a few extra hops once a combo is building.

The board, score, block positions and combo are saved to ysgame-hop-state, your best score to ysgame-hop-best and the chosen level to ysgame-hop-diff — all in your own browser. Close the tab and pick up the same run later (resume where you left off); a new run never clears your best.

Puzzle Cube: the rules

A 2x2 or 3x3 cube sits on the screen, each of its six faces a different colour. Turn one layer at a time and walk every colour back onto its own face — one colour per face and the cube is solved. There is Scramble to start fresh, Undo to step back, Hint to suggest a turn and Reset to put it back to solved. There is no countdown, so take as long as you like; Medium and Hard record your time and moves for the score.

The three levels change the size, how many scramble turns you get, how many hints you have and how the result is scored: Easy is a 2x2 with unlimited hints, Hard a 3x3 scrambled thoroughly and scored on move count.

Controls

Computer: drag the board to rotate the view and see three faces at once; use the layer buttons on the right to turn a layer, and hold Shift to turn it the other way. Keyboard is quicker: U D L R F B each turn a layer, with Shift to reverse, and P pauses and resumes. Z undoes, H gives a hint, the arrow keys rotate the view and Space starts or pauses. The buttons up top undo, scramble, reset and give hints at any time.

Phone or tablet: drag a finger to rotate the view, tap the layer buttons to turn one; undo, scramble and hint are all on-screen buttons.

Three difficulty levels

LevelWhat changesGood for
🌱 Easy2x2, 6 scramble turns, unlimited hintsLearning the six faces and which way each layer turns
🌳 Medium3x3, 14 scramble turns, 3 hints, with a timerYou can solve it and want to get quicker
🔥 Hard3x3, 25 scramble turns, only 1 hint, scored on move countPlanning each turn and avoiding wasted moves

The rules and the turns are identical at every level — only the size and the scramble change. Pick one at the start; switching scrambles a fresh cube.

A small tip: finish one face first (the white base is a good start), then keep it intact and build upwards — far fewer moves than twisting at random. Don't force a wrong turn back by hand; Undo is cleaner. Hard gives just one hint, so save it for a real dead end.

The current cube, move count, timer and hints left are saved to ysgame-cube-state, your best result to ysgame-cube-best and the chosen size to ysgame-cube-diff — all in your own browser. Close the tab and carry on with the same cube; a new one never clears your best.

Billiards: the rules

A table with six pockets. Press on the table and drag away from the shot line to aim — the farther you pull, the harder the shot — then release to strike. The cue ball hits the object balls, balls bounce off one another and off the cushions, and potting an object ball scores. Pot the cue ball yourself and you lose a point. Wait for everything to stop before the next shot; there is no clock, so line it up properly.

The three levels change the number of balls, pocket size, table friction, how much of an aiming guide you get and the shot limit; on Hard, potting the cue ball costs double.

Controls

Computer: press on the table and drag opposite to where you want to shoot; a dashed line shows where the cue ball makes first contact, and releasing fires. Left/Right swing the angle and Up/Down fine-tune it; hold Space to charge and release to strike, P pauses and resumes.

Phone or tablet: press a finger on the table and drag away from the shot line to aim and build power, then let go; the dashed line is your guide while dragging.

Three difficulty levels

LevelWhat changesGood for
🌱 Easy4 balls, wide pockets, low friction, full aiming line, no shot limitLearning the drag power and rebound angles
🌳 Medium6 balls, standard pockets, medium friction, first-contact point only, 24 shotsYou can position the cue ball and want to count shots
🔥 Hard9 balls, narrow pockets, high friction, short guide line, 16 shots, doubled cue-ball foulMaking every shot count, not over-attacking

Every level ends when the table is cleared; pick one before you start, or switch mid-game for a fresh rack.

A small tip: don't pull back at full power straight away. Look at where the dashed line meets the object ball — where the cue ball comes to rest often matters more than whether this shot goes in. A potted cue ball costs a point, double on Hard, so leave a little in reserve.

Ball positions, score and shots left are saved to ysgame-billiards-state, your best to ysgame-billiards-best and the chosen level to ysgame-billiards-diff — all in your own browser. Close the tab and pick up the same rack later; a new one never clears your best.

Xiangqi: the rules

A 9x10 board, the river and the border between the two sides. You play red and move first, the computer plays black, and you take turns. Checkmate the opposing general, or leave it with no legal move (stalemate), and you win. The full movement rules are here: the rook moves in straight lines, the horse moves in an L but can be blocked at the leg, the elephant moves two diagonals, can be blocked at the eye and never crosses the river, the advisor steps one diagonally and stays inside the palace, the two generals may never face each other on an open file, the cannon needs a screen between itself and its target to capture, and the soldier only moves forward until it crosses the river, after which it may also step sideways. When in check you must answer it — you can't ignore it.

Undo takes back one turn, which is two moves — yours and the computer's — returning the board to just before you played. If you're unsure where to go, Hint suggests a move. The three levels change the computer's strength; the rules are identical at every level.

Controls

Computer: tap one of your pieces, then a highlighted square to move; or use the keyboard: Arrow keys move the cursor, Space or Enter selects and places, U undoes, H hints and P pauses and resumes.

Phone or tablet: tap a piece, then a highlighted square; the buttons below the board undo and give hints.

Three difficulty levels

LevelWhat changesGood for
🌱 EasySearch depth 1, material value only, 30% chance of a second-best moveLearning how each piece moves
🌳 MediumSearch depth 2, positional value and alpha-beta pruning, capped at 4,000 nodes per moveA proper back-and-forth game
🔥 HardSearch depth 3, alpha-beta pruning, check and mobility weighting, capped at 20,000 nodes per moveWhen you want to think each move through

The board and rules are identical at every level — only how deeply the computer searches changes. Pick one at the start, or switch mid-game for a fresh round.

A small tip: don't get so busy taking a rook that you forget your own general — the two generals facing each other is illegal, and an unanswered check loses on the spot. Fill out your advisors and elephants and guard the central files early; going out with the rook too soon is far riskier. Save Hint for when you're genuinely stuck.

The board, whose turn it is, the level and the result are saved to ysgame-xiangqi-state, your win count to ysgame-xiangqi-best and the chosen level to ysgame-xiangqi-diff — all in your own browser. Close the tab and resume the same game later; a new one never clears your wins.

Questions

If I close the tab, do I lose my progress?

No. Every move is saved to your browser's local storage and restored when you come back, timer included.

Two things will lose it: clearing this site's data in your browser, or switching device or browser. The save belongs to one browser, and doesn't sync.

Why are my records gone on another device?

Because there's no account system. Progress is stored locally, not in the cloud. That's the trade for not asking you for an email address.

I made a mistake but can't find it

Press Check. Wrong cells turn red. It tells you which ones, not what the answer should be — that part is still yours.

If you're genuinely stuck, Hint fills one cell. You get three per game.

Does it work offline?

The first visit needs a connection. After that, as long as your browser cache is intact, the page will usually still open offline — the game itself doesn't need the network at all.

Are there ads? Do they cover the board?

A few, above and below the content. They're the only income and they pay for the domain and hosting. No pop-ups, nothing over the board, nothing interrupting a game.

Ads are filled by a third party, so I don't choose what appears. See the privacy policy for how the cookies work.

The board doesn't fit on my phone

Try reloading the page first. If it still looks wrong, tell me your phone model and browser and I'll fix it properly.
